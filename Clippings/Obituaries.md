### Jensen Huang, CEO of Nvidia, 1993–2026英伟达公司首席执行官 Jensen Huang，1993 年至 2026 年

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia on Thursday, November 12, 2026. He had been CEO of the company ever since he founded it on April 5, 1993. Nvidia started out as a graphics card company; Huang’s exit — nominally because of affairs with a buyer who may or may not have been a Chinese spy, as well as at least two company employees — was ultimately because Nvidia was, by the powers that be, considered a defense contractor, and Huang never adjusted.  
2026 年 11 月 12 日星期四，Jensen Huang 辞去 Nvidia 公司的首席执行官职务。自 1993 年 4 月 5 日创立该公司以来，他一直担任首席执行官。Nvidia 最初是一家显卡公司；而 Huang 的离职原因，表面上是因为他与一些买家有不正当关系，而这些买家可能也是中国间谍；此外，还有至少两名公司员工也牵涉其中。实际上，Nvidia 被当局视为国防承包商，而 Huang 始终没有做出调整以适应这一变化。

In this, Huang was the ultimate victim of his own success. Nvidia’s first graphics cards simply translated triangles into pixels. The 2001 GeForce 3 introduced a programmable pixel shader, which meant a video card’s functionality was no longer pre-defined. The killer move, however — which almost killed the company — was the 2006 introduction of CUDA, which was an API for programming those shaders. For much of the next 16 years [Huang sold the gospel of CUDA](https://stratechery.com/2021/nvidias-gtc-keynote-the-nvidia-stack-the-omniverse/), convinced it was the platform for a future of accelerated computing; then, in 2022, came ChatGPT.  
在这种情况下，黄浩成了自己成功的牺牲品。英伟达的第一代显卡只是将三角形直接转化为像素。而 2001 年推出的 GeForce 3 显卡引入了可编程像素着色器，这意味着显卡的功能不再固定不变。然而，真正具有革命性的创新是 2006 年推出的 CUDA 技术，它是一种用于编程这些着色器的 API。在接下来的 16 年里，黄浩不断宣传 CUDA 技术，坚信它是未来加速计算技术的基石。直到 2022 年，ChatGPT 的出现才彻底改变了这一局面。

ChatGPT made clear what GPUs were most useful for: large language models, and Nvidia made the best GPUs. More than that, Huang had realized that the future of accelerated computing was not just chips, but rather systems; that drove the purchase of Mellanox in 2019, which provided the technology for Nvidia to scale out from chips, setting it down the road to being the best option for the sort of large scale training that made large language models possible. For this Nvidia charged very high prices, with margins exceeding 70%, because they could: they had the platform, the technology, and, over the past few years as others sought to make their own chips, the supply chain.  
ChatGPT 明确了 GPU 最适用的领域：大规模语言模型的处理。而 Nvidia 则提供了最优质的 GPU。更重要的是，黄先生意识到，加速计算的未来不仅仅是芯片本身，而是整个系统。这一认识促使 Nvidia 在 2019 年购买了 Mellanox 的技術，从而能够將計算能力扩展到芯片之外，为大规模语言模型训练提供了最佳解决方案。由于拥有这样的技术平台，Nvidia 的定价非常高，利润率甚至达到了 70%以上。因为在过去几年里，其他公司试图制造自己的芯片时，Nvidia 已经拥有了完整的供应链。

Huang’s impact on the Asian supply chain in particular cannot be overstated. Byrne Hobart, in his book [Boom: Bubbles and the End of Stagnation](https://www.stripe.press/boom), notes that one of the benefits of bubbles is that they provide a coordination function, as the fear of missing out incentivizes multiple entities with interlocking dependencies to all commit to building at the same time; Huang was, in many respects, a one-person coordination function. Huang gave multiple keynotes a year, which he explained as how he communicated with the ecosystem broadly, and was famous for eating and drinking his way across Asia in particular, convincing reluctant suppliers that the AI boom was real, and locking up the lion’s share of their output for Nvidia and its stunning commitment to a yearly release cycle.  
黄对亚洲供应链的影响是不可忽视的。伯恩·霍巴德在他的著作《泡沫与停滞的终结》中提到，泡沫的一个好处就是它们能够起到协调作用——因为人们都害怕错过机会，所以那些相互依赖的多个实体都会同时投入建设。黄在很多方面就像是一个协调者。他在一年中多次发表演讲，他解释说，他通过与整个生态系统进行沟通来发挥这种协调作用。他以其横跨亚洲的旅行而闻名，他让那些犹豫的供应商相信人工智能的发展是真实存在的，并成功获得了他们的大部分产出，这些产出都被留给了英伟达，英伟达还承诺每年都会发布新的产品。

Huang, who had toiled for decades in relative obscurity, known primarily to gamers, and only barely to the tech industry, became a rock star, swarmed wherever he went, always in his black leather jacket. He also, to his ultimate detriment, became a political figure. Nvidia had long had no presence in either Washington D.C. or [Beijing](https://www.ft.com/content/3efa9733-fbb4-47c2-83ba-b01e548ef277?syn-25a6b1a6=1), and was blindsided by the Biden administration’s imposition of chip controls in 2022. Nvidia’s immediate reaction to those initial controls, which restricted exports based on interconnect speed, was to immediately release the A800 in China, which was an A100 with a third of its interconnects disabled. That set the tone for the conflict that I believe was the true cause of Huang’s downfall: Huang strenuously disagreed with the various iterations of the chip ban, and Nvidia only bothered to do the bare minimum to abide by the letter of the law, never the spirit.  
黄在相对默默无闻的环境中辛苦工作了数十年，他的名声主要只在游戏玩家之中流传，而在科技行业则几乎无人知晓。然而，他后来成为了一名“摇滚明星”，无论走到哪里都受到人们的追捧，而且他总是穿着黑色皮夹克。然而，这对他来说却是致命的缺陷——他逐渐成为了一名政治人物。Nvidia 长期以来在华盛顿特区和北京都几乎没有影响力，而 2022 年拜登政府实施的芯片管制措施让 Nvidia 措手不及。面对这些基于互连速度进行出口限制的规定，Nvidia 的第一反应是在中国发布 A800 芯片，实际上这款芯片就是 A100，只不过其互连功能被禁用了三分之一。这一举动为后续的冲突埋下了伏笔，我认为这才是导致黄垮台的真正原因：黄强烈反对各种版本的芯片禁令，而 Nvidia 则只是表面上遵守了法律，根本没有真正践行法律精神。

That approach, in all likelihood, extended to Nvidia’s oversight (or lack thereof) of Singaporean entities that were alleged to be fronts for chips for China. Some of these were legal: Chinese companies are allowed to access cloud computing facilities outside of China that house leading edge Nvidia chips, although chip control advocates are seeking to close that loophole; some were not legal, in that the chips were ultimately ferried to China. Megaspeed, whose executive, Alice Huang, was the first person Huang was alleged to have an affair with, was officially in the former business, and alleged to actually be in the latter. There are whispers that Megaspeed’s ultimate customer was not, or not just, Alibaba, but also the Chinese military. These are whispers, to be clear; it was the affairs that were enough, at least publicly, to lead to Huang’s resignation.  
这种做法很可能也涉及到了 Nvidia 对新加坡一些实体的监管不力问题——这些实体被指控是向中国输送芯片的幌子公司。其中一些行为是合法的：中国公司可以访问中国境外的云计算设施，而这些设施中存储着 Nvidia 的尖端芯片。不过，一些行为则属于非法行为，因为这些芯片最终都被运到了中国。Megaspeed 公司的首席执行官 Alice Huang，最初被指控与某个人有不正当关系，但实际上该公司似乎同时从事着前者所描述的那项业务。有传言称，Megaspeed 的最终客户不仅仅是阿里巴巴，还有中国军方。不过，这些只是传言而已；至少从公开情况来看，正是这些不正当关系导致了 Huang 的辞职。

### Nvidia, Most Valuable Company in the World, 2024–2026英伟达，2024 至 2026 年全球最具价值的企业

Nvidia’s durability — at least that of their margins — was already an open question. Start with CUDA: when the market was software written for GPUs, a platform — including not just an API but a huge set of libraries targeting every conceivable market for accelerated computing — was a nearly impenetrable moat. That is why LLMs like GPT were written using CUDA.  
Nvidia 的竞争力——至少是其利润率方面——其实一直是个未知数。以 CUDA 为例：当市场还只是针对 GPU 开发的软件时，一个包含 API 以及大量针对各种加速计算需求的库的集合体，几乎构成了一道难以逾越的屏障。正因如此，像 GPT 这样的语言模型才采用 CUDA 进行开发。

LLMs, though, both brought Nvidia its incredible success, and also seeded its greatest risks. The first is that the vast majority of developer energy moved up the stack, using the LLM’s API as a platform; for most companies, CUDA didn’t matter. The second is that Anthropic, which found funding from Amazon and Google, originally also-rans to the Microsoft-OpenAI partnership, had to learn how to use Trainium chips from Amazon and TPUs from Google; this was difficult, but ultimately meant that the leading LLM did not have any Nvidia dependency at all (Anthropic runs on Nvidia chips, because they learned how to run on anything). The third is the most devastating: LLMs are so effective at writing software that any software-based moat will inevitably dry up.  
不过，这些大型语言模型不仅为 Nvidia 带来了巨大的成功，同时也带来了最大的风险。第一个风险是，绝大多数开发者都选择利用大型语言模型的 API 作为开发平台，而 CUDA 对于大多数公司来说并不重要。第二个风险是，Anthropic 这家从亚马逊和谷歌那里获得资金的公司，原本与 Microsoft-OpenAI 的合作关系并不稳固。他们不得不学习如何使用亚马逊的 Trainium 芯片以及谷歌的 TPU 芯片。这确实很困难，但最终意味着那些领先的大型语言模型完全不再依赖 Nvidia 的硬件支持（Anthropic 使用 Nvidia 芯片进行运行，因为他们已经学会了如何在任何硬件上运行）。第三个风险最为严重：大型语言模型在编写软件方面表现得非常出色，以至于任何基于软件的竞争优势都将不可避免地消失。

Nvidia’s second moat, its networking and systems approach, is more durable, although challenged by Google (and by extension, Anthropic) in particular. Nvidia’s systems approach is also the easy answer for enterprises and governments seeking to own their own hardware, and neoclouds seeking to build data centers that are independent of the hyperscalers. The latter market rents chips to both hyperscalers and frontier labs, as well as individual companies, but is challenged as obvious sources of power and political permission disappear. They also need money to afford Nvidia’s systems, which has increasingly come from Nvidia itself.  
Nvidia 的第二种商业模式，即网络与系统解决方案，虽然较为成熟，但仍然面临来自谷歌的竞争压力（以及 Anthropic 公司的挑战）。对于希望拥有自己硬件的企业和政府来说，Nvidia 的系统解决方案是一个理想的解决方案。而对于那些希望建立独立于大型云提供商的数据中心的新云服务提供商来说，Nvidia 的解决方案同样具有吸引力。不过，随着那些明显的权力来源和政治支持逐渐消失，这一市场也面临着挑战。此外，这些新云服务提供商还需要资金来购买 Nvidia 的硬件产品，而这些资金越来越多地来自 Nvidia 自身。

Nvidia’s third moat is its mastery of the supply chain, and this is the moat that suddenly looks much more tenuous. Huang has bragged about the fact that he has never signed a contract with TSMC over their three decades of partnership; it’s a relationship built on a handshake and mutual trust with Huang specifically. This is, per the point above, a very Asian way of doing business, and Huang was the master of that. It’s questionable whether or not that trust and willingness to commit will persist when the handshake is no longer there.  
Nvidia 的第三个优势在于其对供应链的掌控能力。不过，这一优势现在似乎变得不那么稳固了。黄氏曾自豪地表示，在长达三十年的合作关系中，他从未与台积电签订过任何合同；这种合作关系是基于个人之间的信任和默契而建立的。正如上文所述，这种商业模式属于典型的亚洲式经营方式，而黄氏正是这种模式的大师。不过，当这种信任和合作基础不再存在时，这种关系能否持续下去，就值得怀疑了。

Nvidia’s most important moat, however, was the degree to which it was not a normal company. Huang organized Nvidia as an exoskeleton around one man: himself. There is very little management at Nvidia — Huang famously has 60 direct reports — for the same reason that there was no succession plan: Nvidia could move so quickly and so aggressively because the company still behaved as if it were a startup. Indeed, lots of established companies — including those still led by founders — claim to still be a startup, but only one, Nvidia, really was organized that way. That paid off with all of those successful bets; it also created an environment where there were no checks on Huang. And, now that he is gone, the exoskeleton is much more poised to collapse than you might expect.  
不过，Nvidia 最重要的优势在于它并非一家普通的公司。黄仁轩将 Nvidia 作为自己的人身依附体系来管理。Nvidia 几乎没有管理层——据传黄仁轩直接管理着 60 名员工——之所以如此，是因为公司始终保持着初创公司的特性，没有明确的继任计划。事实上，许多成熟的企业——包括那些仍由创始人领导的公司——都自诩为初创公司，但实际上只有 Nvidia 真正如此运作。这一优势带来了诸多成功，同时也创造了一个没有制约黄仁轩的环境。而现在，随着他的离开，这个依附体系也即将崩溃了。

### The AI Bubble, 2025–2027?人工智能泡沫，2025–2027 年？

The first part of the AI buildout was funded by free cash flow, particularly from the hyperscalers. Then, during the second half of last year, nearly everyone started turning to debt. Now — [starting with Google this past summer](https://stratechery.com/2026/the-google-capital-company/) — the primary funding mechanism is equity. In the long run, this should come full circle, and future build-outs will once again be funded by free cash flow, generated from the infrastructure that is being built now.  
该人工智能系统的建设初期是由自由现金流来资助的，其中大部分资金来自大型云计算公司。到了去年下半年，几乎所有人都开始依赖债务融资。而现在——从今年夏天开始，主要的融资方式变成了股权融资。从长远来看，这种融资方式应该会形成一个循环，未来的建设将再次由目前正在建设的基础设施所产生的自由现金流来资助。

The problem for Nvidia is that the companies most able to navigate this progression are the hyperscalers, which have the resources to challenge Nvidia’s hold on the chip market. To that end, Nvidia has been very eager to ensure that it is selling not only to the hyperscalers, but to other would-be competitors, like the neoclouds; there will likely, at some point, be serious questions raised about exactly how Nvidia went about allocating compute in pursuit of this goal.  
Nvidia 面临的问题是，最能够应对这一形势的公司就是那些大型云服务提供商。这些公司拥有足够的资源来挑战 Nvidia 在芯片市场中的主导地位。因此，Nvidia 非常希望不仅将产品销售给大型云服务提供商，同时也销售给其他潜在的竞争对手，比如新云公司。不过，随着时间的推移，人们可能会对 Nvidia 为了实现这一目标而如何分配计算资源提出质疑。

The neoclouds, however, are at a meaningful disadvantage when it comes to funding. First, their debt is significantly more expensive, given that their only business is renting GPUs (as opposed to, say, running the most valuable advertising businesses in the world, or the world’s largest e-commerce platform, or dominating enterprise software). Second, their equity is increasingly worthless, given the significant risks attached to that credit. If a neocloud can’t pay, it’s the equity holders that get wiped out first.  
然而，在资金方面，新云公司确实处于明显的劣势。首先，他们的债务成本要高得多，因为他们唯一的业务就是出租 GPU，而不是从事那些价值连城的广告业务、全球最大的电子商务平台，或是占据企业软件市场的领先地位。其次，他们的股权越来越没有价值了，因为相关的信用风险非常大。如果新云公司无法支付债务，那么首先会被清算的就是股权持有者。

This led to what I termed [Nvidia’s Risky Business](https://stratechery.com/2026/nvidias-risky-business/): Nvidia increasingly used its cash flow and credit rating to backstop and fund the companies who were buying its chips. Bears called this circular financing; bulls called it a necessary bridge to a future where AI revenue paid down these companies’ debt and drove massive upside. What is undoubtedly true is that this was the sort of risky business that is uniquely possible for a multi-trillion dollar company that is completely controlled by one man, who has made an entire career of pushing the limits and making risky bets — and seeing them pay off.

It’s also a series of bets that are unlikely to continue. The neoclouds should be very nervous about losing their patron, along with their creditors and equity holders. The other problem is that the revenue necessary to make all of this build-out work is something on the order of a trillion dollars industry-wide, and it needs to materialize sooner rather than later; if it falls short, it’s the hyperscalers who will prioritize their own compute first, and they will likely be seen as a safe haven for everyone committing to spending. In that world, Nvidia’s neocloud hedge — absent Huang’s willingness to shore the ecosystem up — is liable to turn into a millstone, with their assets flooding the market even as the supply chain ramp-up brings ever more supply to market.

### AI as Normal Technology and the U.S. Semiconductor Industry, 1947~2047

Perhaps because he had been preaching the gospel of accelerated computing for so long, Huang was the leading figure pushing back against the doomer narrative surrounding AI. Yes, AI was a massive business opportunity, but it was still normal technology; the safety issues presented were engineering challenges to be solved. What required consideration was not just the next five years, but the next fifty. It’s a view that I am, in full disclosure, generally aligned with.

Many others are not. On one hand there are the aforementioned doomers, certain that fast takeoff is imminent — or has already started — as AI increasingly builds better AI in a recursive loop. The end state is an AI that is massively more capable than humans, and uncontrollable by them; the only hope is to ensure that AI is aligned with humanity, by which adherents to this viewpoint mean aligned with them and their view of the world. This contingent is, broadly speaking, represented by Anthropic. And, in this view, actively working to enable and accelerate uncontrollable open weights models is a threat to humanity.

On the other hand there are the China hawks, who view AI as a national security issue, and the key to U.S. military dominance in a world where China owns the means of production of most militarily relevant hardware. In this world giving China access to chips is effectively treason against the United States.  
另一方面，还有一些中国鹰派人士认为人工智能是一个国家安全问题，他们认为掌握人工智能技术是美国在军事上保持主导地位的关键。在这样一个世界中，如果中国能够获取芯片技术，那就相当于对美国进行叛逃行为。

For both of these groups, Huang was public enemy number one, because of his determination to do what was best for Nvidia’s business, which was to sell chips to China. This made sense for Nvidia for multiple reasons:  
对于这两个群体来说，黄仁轩都是头号公敌。因为他始终致力于为英伟达在中国的业务谋福利，也就是努力向中国销售英伟达的芯片。对英伟达而言，这样做有多个原因：

- The first and most obvious reason is that it is good for a company to be able to sell to everyone, including the second largest market in the world (China has traditionally made up about a quarter of Nvidia’s sales, long before AI was even a thing).  
	第一个也是最明显的原因是，对于一家公司来说，能够向所有人销售产品是非常有益的。尤其是面对全球第二大市场——中国时，这一点更为重要（在人工智能技术出现之前，中国就已经占据了 Nvidia 销售额的约四分之一）。
- The second is that China is a large enough market, with sufficiently motivated and capable developers, that it can actually create a meaningful CUDA alternative; furthermore, such an alternative would, in the long run, not be constrained to China, but rather compete with Nvidia all over the world.  
	其次，中国是一个规模足够大的市场，拥有足够积极且能力强大的开发者。因此，中国完全可以打造出一个具有竞争力的 CUDA 替代产品。而且，从长远来看，这样的替代产品不会局限于中国境内，而是可以在全球范围内与英伟达竞争。
- The third is that China’s AI ecosystem, unconstrained as it is by such niceties as terms of service, distills frontier lab models and releases them as open weights models; those models, meanwhile, are tuned to run on Nvidia chips. These models give Nvidia leverage versus the frontier labs all over the world, including in the West.  
	第三，中国的 AI 生态系统不受诸如服务条款之类的繁琐条款限制，能够提炼出前沿的实验室模型，并将其作为开放式的模型发布出去。这些模型可以运行在 Nvidia 的芯片上。这样的做法让 Nvidia 在全球范围内，包括西方地区，都拥有了竞争优势。
- The fourth is that China is uniquely positioned to create a fundamentally different approach to systems design using optical interconnects to scale out training clusters. This is much more inefficient in terms of power usage than Nvidia’s more elegant designs, but China is also much more capable of manufacturing excess amounts of power to make it work.  
	第四点是，中国拥有独特的优势，能够创造出一种与现有系统设计截然不同的方法。通过采用光学互连技术来扩展训练集群的规模，这种方式在功耗方面要远低于英伟达那些更先进的解决方案。不过，中国在制造能力方面也更为强大，能够生产出足够的电力来支持这种方式的实施。
- The fifth is that, in the very long run, China can create an entire alternative supply chain for semiconductors broadly, rendering Nvidia’s mastery of the Western-aligned supply chain much less important.  
	第五点是，从长远来看，中国可以独自建立起一个完整的半导体替代供应链，这样一来，Nvidia 对西方标准供应链的掌控就变得不那么重要了。

This last point is worth dwelling on. The chief objection that China hawks in particular make to the idea that it is a good idea to sell China chips in order to prevent them from surpassing the U.S. is that China is already heavily invested in building their own ecosystem. This is certainly true, but I think it underplays the complexity of chips and what is necessary to actually compete with the West.  
最后一个要点值得仔细考虑。那些认为通过出售芯片来阻止中国超越美国的观点，中国的一些批评者主要提出的反对意见是：中国已经在构建自己的芯片生态系统方面投入了大量资源。这一点确实正确，但我认为这种观点低估了芯片领域的复杂性，以及中国要真正与西方竞争所需付出的努力。

Specifically, the Chinese government can only control the supply side, by spending money and making top-down imperatives; building an ecosystem, however, whether that be for AI specifically, or semiconductors broadly, requires demand as well. Progress in models and systems and chips depends on a feedback loop between the user and the developer, or the customer and the manufacturer. The demand side, however, is much more short-term oriented than the Chinese government might prefer. The model companies’ concern is the other model companies: that means if they are distilling, we must distill too. If they are using Nvidia chips, then we must too. This matters up and down the stack, just as it has always mattered in technology, which really is different in this regard from almost every other industry.  
具体来说，中国政府只能控制供应方面，通过投入资金并制定自上而下的指令来实现这一目标。然而，无论是针对人工智能还是半导体产业，建立完善的生态系统都需要需求方面的支持。模型和系统以及芯片的进展取决于用户与开发者之间的互动关系。不过，需求方面则更注重短期效益，这可能与中国政府的态度有所不同。模式公司的关注点在于其他模式公司：也就是说，如果他们采用某种技术或芯片，我们也必须采用同样的技术或芯片。这一点在科技领域非常重要，因为科技行业与其他行业相比，在这方面有着本质的不同。

A useful analogy is to consider the Internet. Why is it that country after country can profess a desire to have an indigenous alternative to the American tech giants, yet miserably fail in every attempt to generate it? It’s because the tech giants are only a click away! In this regard, China’s Great Firewall was one of the most brilliant geostrategic moves ever: by virtue of cutting off access to the American tech companies, China created the demand that pulled China’s own tech giants into existence. You need demand, not just desire.  
一个有趣的类比就是互联网。为什么一个又一个国家都声称希望拥有自己的科技企业来替代美国的科技巨头，但却在尝试创建这些企业的过程中屡屡失败呢？这是因为科技巨头的服务几乎触手可及！在这方面，中国的“防火墙”政策堪称最出色的地缘战略举措之一：通过限制与美国科技公司的联系，中国创造了需求，从而催生了属于自己的科技企业。我们需要的是需求，而不仅仅是愿望。

This is why China itself is currently banning the import of Nvidia’s two-generations-behind H20 chips, after the Trump administration foolishly banned their import, only to reverse themselves while bragging about the reason to do so — i.e. keep China addicted to Nvidia. China is replaying the Great Firewall playbook: force its internal demand to use its much worse alternatives, because that is the only way those alternatives become better. And, in the long run, those alternatives, and the entire ecosystem that supports them, will flood the world.  
这就是为什么中国目前正在禁止进口英伟达那些技术落后的 H20 芯片。在特朗普政府愚蠢地禁止这些芯片进口之后，中国又改变了立场，还自夸这样做是为了让中国继续依赖英伟达。中国其实是在效仿“防火墙”的做法：迫使国内需求使用那些质量更差的替代品，因为只有这样，这些替代品才能变得更好。而从长远来看，这些替代品以及支持它们的整个生态系统将会蔓延到全世界。

Huang was, to be perfectly clear, talking his book when it came to his insistence on selling chips to China, or, in the case of the current controversy, allegedly turning a blind eye towards nominally legal buyers who were intent on violating the chip ban; that doesn’t mean, however, that he was wrong about the long-term effects, at least if AI is a normal technology (and I haven’t gotten to [the risks this imposes on the Taiwan Strait](https://stratechery.com/2025/ai-promise-and-chip-precariousness/)). What is clear now, however, is that the doomers and the hawks have won: AI, at least in terms of policy, will be treated as anything but normal, and in the fullness of time, it is the U.S. chip ecosystem that will pay the ultimate price.  
需要明确的是，黄在谈到坚持向中国出售芯片的问题时，确实提出了自己的观点。在当前的争议中，他似乎对那些试图违反芯片禁令的所谓“合法”买家视而不见。不过，这并不意味着他对人工智能的长期影响有误解——至少如果人工智能成为一种常规技术的话（我还没有考虑过这对台湾海峡地区所带来的风险）。不过，现在可以肯定的是，那些悲观者和保守派已经取得了胜利：至少在政策层面上，人工智能将不会被当作常规技术来对待。最终，美国的芯片生态系统将会付出最大的代价。

### Tech Company Independence, 1968–2026科技公司的独立性，1968–2026 年

The birth date for the last obituary was the invention of the transistor; the birth date for this one is the founding of Intel. Robert Noyce and co-founders had spent the last decade at Fairchild Semiconductor, but were constrained by their parent company’s fundamental conservatism and chafed at their lack of upside. Arthur Rock effectively created the venture capital industry in Silicon Valley through his financing of Intel; Noyce created the concept of the commercial-market-led technology company that made it up in volume. At that time the largest single purchaser of chips was the U.S. government; Noyce realized that the fastest way to progress was to make chips for everyone, including the government, and on his terms, not theirs.  
最后一则讣告的发布日期是晶体管的发明时间；而这一则讣告的发布日期则是英特尔的成立时间。罗伯特·诺伊斯及其联合创始人过去十年都在飞兆半导体公司工作，但他们受到母公司保守理念的束缚，感到非常不满，因为他们看不到晋升的机会。亚瑟·罗克通过为英特尔提供融资支持，真正开创了硅谷的风险投资产业；诺伊斯则提出了以商业市场为导向的技术公司模式，从而实现了规模上的突破。当时，最大的芯片采购方是美国政府；诺伊斯意识到，取得进展的最快途径就是按照自己的标准来生产芯片，而不是按照政府的标准来生产。

From that point on, tech as an industry operated with the assumption that the world was its customer, and that their own executives defined the route to progress. Government was free to buy their products, but not to define them, and if they wanted the best products, then they best not restrict who they could sell them to either. That’s how important volume was, and, over time, it became clear how powerful and essential ecosystems were.  
从那时起，科技行业就以一种假设为基础来运作——即世界就是他们的客户，而企业的高管们则负责决定发展的方向。政府可以购买他们的产品，但无权对其进行限制；如果希望获得最好的产品，那么就不应该限制可以销售给谁。这就是规模的重要性所在。随着时间的推移，人们逐渐意识到生态系统是多么强大且不可或缺的力量。

Other industries operate differently; I thought this article in the Financial Times was striking, and not just because of the headline: [Bessent says Nvidia needs ‘shake-up’ with external CEO](https://www.ft.com/content/24edbb84-7520-4ef0-a57c-30359e12ccc5?syn-25a6b1a6=1):  
其他行业的情况则有所不同；我觉得《金融时报》这篇报道非常引人注目，不仅仅是因为它的标题：贝森特表示，英伟达需要有一位新的首席执行官来推动公司的变革。

> Bessent said that it is important “to examine whether Nvidia had a culture of compliance around the US export restrictions” and whether its top executives knew they were being skirted. “Was it a low-level fail? Was it in the middle, or was it up in the C-suite? And who knew? These shell companies were obvious, they were being investigated, and did Nvidia use them as a workaround to sell chips to the Chinese?” he said. “Were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?” The Treasury secretary added that Nvidia needs to provide answers within a matter of days: “I think we need answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel”. He added that “we will see if Nvidia intentionally ignored the warning signs of these shell companies, many of them in Singapore”.  
> 贝森特表示，有必要“调查尼吉比特是否拥有相关合规文化，以遵守美国的出口限制规定”，以及其高层管理人员是否意识到自己被违反这些规定。“这是一次低级失误吗？还是属于中等程度的违规行为？还是说问题出在高层管理团队？谁知道呢？这些空壳公司很明显是被调查的对象，尼吉比特是否利用它们作为手段来向中国销售芯片呢？”他说：“这些看似非法的销售行为，是否是为了增加收入和利润而进行的？”财政部长补充说，尼吉比特需要在几天内给出答案：“我认为我们需要在接下来的 10 天内得到董事会和外部法律顾问的回应。”他还指出：“我们将看看尼吉比特是否故意忽视了这些空壳公司的警示信号，其中许多公司位于新加坡。”
> 
> Bessent has pointed the spotlight on Howard Lutnick’s commerce department for failing to ban exports of Nvidia’s chips to Megaspeed, the Singaporean company linked to the alleged Chinese spy who had an affair with Huang. US authorities have been investigating whether Megaspeed, which builds AI data centres in Asia, has been involved in smuggling high-end Nvidia chips, which are powering the AI boom, into China. Bessent has blamed the commerce department more than once this week for failing to ban exports of Nvidia’s chips to Megaspeed. Commerce delayed efforts to add Megaspeed to Washington’s blacklist of companies the US considers a risk to national security. “Many other departments were pushing for this, and commerce had the pen,” Bessent repeated on Sunday. “We have all the rules we need. We just need them enforced.”  
> 贝森特多次指责霍华德·卢特尼克所在的商务部门未能禁止将英伟达的芯片出口给与黄有关的、涉嫌参与中国间谍活动的新加坡公司 Megaspeed。美国当局正在调查这家在亚洲建造人工智能数据中心的公司是否参与了将英伟达高端芯片走私到中国的行为。本周，贝森特多次指责商务部门未能阻止英伟达芯片对 Megaspeed 的出口。商务部门推迟了将 Megaspeed 列入美国国家安全风险企业黑名单的进程。“许多其他部门都在推动这件事，而商务部门则有权力这么做，”贝森特在周日重申道。“我们拥有所有必要的规则，只是需要执行这些规则。”
> 
> Lutnick is under mounting pressure from within the administration and from Congress, which has demanded that he explain how he allowed chips to be exported to a company linked to an alleged Chinese spy. US lawmakers are also accelerating efforts to crack down on advanced AI chips reaching China. If Nvidia “were a bank and they hadn’t complied like this, the \[Treasury’s Office of the Comptroller of the Currency\] would be in there right now, and a lot of the executives would be removed”, Bessent said.  
> 卢特尼克面临着来自政府内部以及国会的日益增大的压力。国会要求他解释为何允许将芯片出口给一家与所谓的中国间谍有关联的公司。美国立法者也正在加紧努力，以打击那些被运往中国的先进人工智能芯片。贝森特说：“如果英伟达‘像银行一样’遵守规定，那么财政部货币监理署现在就不会介入此事，许多高管也会被免职。”

Scott Bessent is the Treasury Secretary, which is to say that he operates an apparatus that is arguably just as, if not more, powerful than the U.S. military. Treasury controls the flow of money around the world, and its soldiers are the banks; if Treasury has sanctioned an entity, then it is up to banks to ensure those sanctions are enforced. They need to know their customers, and they need to analyze every dollar flowing through their systems; if they do not, they are breaking the law. Ignorance is not a defense.  
斯科特·贝森特是财政部长，也就是说，他掌控着一个或许比美国军队更强大的权力机构。财政部负责全球的资金流动，而旗下的银行则构成了他的“军队”。一旦财政部对某家机构实施制裁，银行就有责任确保这些制裁得到执行。他们必须了解自己的客户，并且要分析每一笔通过他们系统的资金流动情况；否则，他们就是在违法。无知根本无法成为借口。

Ignorance, on the other hand, has been tech’s defense basically forever. Yes, there are bad people in the world, which means that if the whole world is using your products, they will be used to do bad things. That was considered an acceptable trade-off, however, because most people are good and the benefits that come from scale — and an avoidance of the friction that comes from trying to track the activities of billions of people — were worth the risk.  
而另一方面，无知一直都是技术的一种防御手段。当然，世界上确实存在坏人，但既然全世界都在使用你的产品，那么这些产品也可能会被用于做坏事。不过，这种权衡被认为是可以接受的，因为大多数人都是善良的，而且从规模化的优势中获得的收益——以及避免因追踪数十亿人的活动而产生的麻烦——都使得这种风险是值得的。

This was certainly what Huang believed, and, to his detriment, he aggressively belittled anyone who dared disagree (the extent to which Huang is despised in Washington D.C., as evidenced by the giddiness and bloodlust online last week, is extraordinary). In Huang’s view, as long as the Commerce Department said that it was ok to sell to Megaspeed, then it was ok to sell to Megaspeed — and to sleep with one of its executives.  
这无疑是黄的观点。然而，对他来说不幸的是，他总是贬低那些持不同意见的人。黄在华盛顿特区非常不受欢迎，这一点从上周网上出现的狂热和恶意评论中可以看出来。在黄看来，只要商务部表示向 Megaspeed 销售是合法的，那么向 Megaspeed 销售就是合法的——当然，也可以与他们的高管们发生关系。

Bessent views the matter entirely differently: he thinks that Nvidia should operate like a bank. It’s not just their job to abide by the letter of the law, but the spirit of it. It’s not the government’s job to enforce the chip ban, but the company’s. An attestation from a Singaporean customer that chips aren’t going to China isn’t worth anything; actually putting boots on the ground and ensuring compliance is the only way Nvidia cannot be in violation of the U.S. government’s dictates.  
贝森则持有完全不同的看法：他认为英伟达应该像银行一样运作。遵守法律条文固然重要，但更重要的是要遵循法律的精神。执行芯片禁令并不是政府的职责，而是公司的责任。来自新加坡客户的声明表明他们不会将芯片运往中国，但这毫无意义；真正重要的是要付诸实践，确保合规——只有这样，英伟达才能避免违反美国政府的指令。

Again, for tech, this is extraordinary, even if it is normal in banking. Extraordinary enough, in fact, that Huang is being labeled by many — not just in Washington, but also in the finance world — as a traitor. Huang, in my estimation, is just a businessman who cares about nothing else other than Nvidia; that worked out great when he made GPUs for gaming that no one in government cared about or understood. That’s just a normal technology; when AI was deemed to be anything but normal, however, business as usual wasn’t just over: it was a crime, and it cost Huang his job — and maybe more.  
再次强调，在科技领域，这种情况确实非常罕见。即便在银行业这种领域也是如此。事实上，黄氏被许多人视为叛徒——这些人的观点不仅存在于华盛顿，也存在于金融界。在我看来，黄氏不过是一个只关心英伟达业务的商人而已。当他开发出那些政府官员们根本不关心或不理解的游戏显卡时，他的表现相当出色。这毕竟只是一项普通的科技成就。然而，当人工智能被视为非普通事物时，一切就变得复杂了：这被视为一种犯罪行为，而黄氏因此失去了工作——或许还有更多东西。

It’s worth pointing out that while this episode was shocking, I should not, in retrospect, have been surprised. Earlier this year, when Anthropic faced off against the U.S. government, I wrote in [Anthropic and Alignment](https://stratechery.com/2026/anthropic-and-alignment/):  
需要指出的是，虽然这一集的情节令人震惊，但回顾起来，我其实并不应该感到惊讶。今年早些时候，当 Anthropic 与美国政府对抗时，我曾在《Anthropic and Alignment》一书中写过相关内容。

> At the same time, what is the standard by which it should be decided what is allowed and not allowed if not laws, which are passed by an elected Congress? Anthropic’s position is that Amodei — who I am using as a stand-in for Anthropic’s management and its board — ought to decide what its models are used for, despite the fact that Amodei is not elected and not accountable to the public.  
> 同时，如果不存在由民选的国会通过的法律法规来界定什么是允许的行为、什么是禁止的行为，那么应该用什么标准来决定这些行为的界限呢？Anthropic 认为，Amodei——我将其作为 Anthropic 管理和董事会的代理人物——应该决定其模型应该用于什么目的。尽管 Amodei 并非民选人员，也不对公众负责。
> 
> And, on the second point, who decides when and in what way American military capabilities are used? That is the responsibility of the Department of War, which ultimately answers to the President, who also is elected. Once again, however, Anthropic’s position is that an unaccountable Amodei can unilaterally restrict what its models are used for.  
> 其次，谁来决定美国军事力量应该以何种方式、在何时被使用呢？这一责任属于战争部，而战争部的最终决策权在于总统，因为总统同样是通过选举产生的。不过，Antropic 认为，一个不负责任的 Amodei 可以单方面决定这些模型的应用范围。

My point in that Article was that if AI is as exceptional as claimed, then the government will demand a say in how it is used, whether or not private company executives agree; moreover, that was a good thing, or at least a least-bad thing. The same analysis applies to Nvidia and Huang: Huang may have fundamentally disagreed with the chip ban, but at the end of the day, it was his duty to accept that it was the law of the land. To the extent he did not do so is ultimately his responsibility.  
我在那篇文章中提出的观点是，如果人工智能真的如所声称的那样出色，那么政府就会要求对它的使用方式有所发言权，无论私营公司的高管是否同意这一点。此外，这其实是一件好事，或者至少算是不坏的事情。同样的分析也适用于英伟达和黄氏：黄氏可能根本不同意这项芯片禁令，但归根结底，他必须接受这是国家的法律。如果他没有这样做，那最终责任就由他自己承担。

### American Exceptionalism, 1776–2026美国的例外主义，1776–2026 年

Perhaps the most tragic aspect of the Huang affair is the extent to which he was the personification of The American Dream. Huang was sent to America as a nine year old who didn’t speak a lick of English, and somehow ended up at a boarding school in rural Kentucky for misbehaving youth, where he would trade help on homework for protection from bullies. From there he went to a state school in Oregon, became an engineer, before famously ending up at a Denny’s diner where he and his co-founders decided to start Nvidia. Then, over the following 33 years, he took risk after risk, created innovation after innovation, until he became a hero not just in the United States, but across the world.  
或许，黄事件中最悲剧的一面在于：他几乎成为了“美国梦”的化身。黄在九岁时被送到美国，当时他一口英语都不会说。后来他被送到肯塔基州的一个寄宿学校，因为行为不端而被关押在那里。在那里，他为了躲避霸凌，不得不放弃帮助完成作业的工作，以换取保护。之后，他进入俄勒冈州的一所公立学校学习，最终成为了一名工程师。后来，他和几位合伙人一起创立了 Nvidia 公司。在接下来的 33 年里，他不断冒险、不断创新，最终成为了不仅在美国，而且在全球范围内都备受尊敬的人物。

And then, you might say, he flew too close to the sun. Certainly he did so in his private life: it’s not just the affairs, which may be par for the course for rich and powerful men; rather, it’s notable just how reckless the affairs were. The ones that were revealed last week were with a customer and potential Chinese spy, and two of his own employees; both are fireable offenses. Meanwhile, the way in which Huang treated his opponents — including actively seeking to get people fired who he felt stood in the way of getting his way — bore the marks of a man who has constructed a life, and a company, where no one can or will stand up to him. That is a recipe for disaster, and here we are.  
然后，你可能会说，他过于接近权力中心了。当然，他在私生活中也如此行事：不仅仅是那些私事，对富人和有权势的人来说，这种行为或许很常见；但问题是，他的行为实在是太过鲁莽了。上周被曝光的一些行为，都是他与某个客户以及一位潜在的中国间谍发生的关系，还有两位他的员工也涉及其中；这些都是严重的违规行为。与此同时，黄对待对手的方式——甚至不惜解雇那些他认为会阻碍他实现计划的人——显示出他是一个能够让所有人都无法或不愿反抗的人。这种心态注定会导致灾难，而我们现在看到的，正是这种灾难的结局。

At the same time, it really does bum me out that what ultimately doomed Huang was a set of rules that really are a big departure from the status quo — and, in my opinion, misguided. Huang’s actions — or lack of actions, in terms of enforcing compliance with the chip ban — are treated as if they are an obvious crime, when in fact this is a dramatic shift in expectations from a short time ago (although there is certainly a callback to previous controversies around selling advanced computing to adversaries). Huang should have adjusted and accepted the new reality, make no mistake; I’m sympathetic to the fact he couldn’t, and recognize the failure in not having structures in place that could have warned him about the dangerous road he was embarking on, even as I realize Huang’s willingness to navigate dangerous roads is precisely why he was an American success story.  
同时，令人沮丧的是，最终导致黄浩失败的，其实是一套与现状严重背离的规则——在我看来，这些规则是误入歧途的。黄浩的行为，或者说他未能执行芯片禁令的行为，被当作明显的犯罪行为来对待。实际上，这种行为的出现，是预期发生的巨大变化，与不久前的情况相比有了显著的不同（尽管肯定也有对之前向对手出售先进计算设备的争议的呼应）。黄浩本应该适应新的现实，接受这一现状。不过，我理解他无法做到这一点，也承认他缺乏能够预警他即将走上这条危险道路的机制。不过，我也意识到，正是黄浩愿意冒险走这条危险之路，才使他成为了一名成功的美国人。

Beyond Huang, however, there is the question of how we compete with China, and I am disappointed, although not surprised, that we are pursuing the route of bans and forced compliance, instead of innovation and openness. Make no mistake, I am no China dove, and I have been very early to the argument that we erred greatly in willingly exporting entire industries to the communist state. That, however, makes it even more imperative that we sprint in the opposite direction of China, towards more freedom in a bet on inventing new industries, not more control and a focus on gatekeeping what we have. I get the logic, particularly in the case of AI, which is extraordinary; I lament the implications of what it says about our belief in ourselves and our ability to stay ahead.  
不过，除了黄之外，还有一个问题就是如何与中国竞争。虽然有些失望，但也不奇怪，我们选择了禁止和强制遵守的做法，而不是依靠创新和开放来竞争。请不要误解我的意思，我并非中国的支持者。我很早以前就认为，我们主动将整个产业出口给这个共产主义国家是一个巨大的错误。然而，这就更加需要我们朝着与中国相反的方向前进，追求更多的自由，努力创造新的产业，而不是寻求更多的控制，或者只关注如何保留我们所拥有的东西。我理解这种逻辑，尤其是在人工智能领域，它确实非常了不起。不过，我对此感到遗憾，因为它反映了我们对自己的信心以及我们保持领先的能力。
